# SOURCES — where the authoritative law lives (start here when verifying)

A species-agnostic directory of WHERE to find primary law and reliable corroboration, plus the order to check
things in. Use it as the starting point for the verify step (RUNBOOK §6) and when sanity-checking a research
result. Everything is listed by ROLE, not as a fragile deep-link list, so it does not rot. Cite the official
primary source for every claim; an agency's written answer outranks any research summary.

## The layer checklist — what to check, in order
Most jurisdictions answer "can I keep / breed / sell / move this species?" across a few predictable layers.
Check them in order and you rarely miss the operative rule.

**A sub-national region (US state, Canadian province, Australian state, German Land, Swiss canton, ...):**
1. The **wildlife / natural-resources agency's species lists** (prohibited / restricted / permitted, or an
   "unregulated" residual). This usually decides KEEP. ⚠️ Tell apart a CLOSED positive list (only listed species
   allowed, so an unlisted species is *prohibited* by default, often permit-ineligible) from an OPEN list (only
   listed species banned, so an unlisted species is *unregulated/legal*). Do not assume "not listed" = legal.
2. The **agriculture / animal-health department's import + movement rules** (entry permit, Certificate of
   Veterinary Inspection / health certificate). Usually governs TRANSPORT, sometimes SELL.
3. Any **commercial breeder / dealer / pet-shop licensing.** Governs SELL/BREED at scale (often dogs/cats only —
   check whether it reaches the species at all).
4. The **national/federal layer** on top (below), and **municipal** ordinances at the bottom.

**A country:**
1. The **national animal-welfare / wildlife / biosecurity law** and any **invasive-species or positive/negative list.**
   ⚠️ For a **non-native / not-established** species, do not stop at the named lists. Many alien-species acts have a
   **general clause covering any alien species** on top of the lists. Read its verb: most ban only **release /
   introduction into the wild** (keeping a caged pet stays legal, so unlisted = legal), but a minority extend it to
   **keeping itself** (Finland's Invasive Species Act §3; Norway's Exotic Animal Regulation positive list), making an
   *unlisted* species prohibited to keep. "Not on the list" ≠ legal until you have read that general clause.
2. Any **supranational layer** it belongs to (e.g. the EU Invasive Alien Species Union list applies across all EU members).
   Note the EU Regulation 1143/2014 keeping ban reaches only **Union-list** species; a member state's *general* alien-
   species keeping ban (if any) is **national** law, so check the national act, not just the EU list.
3. **Import/export:** the national veterinary-import rules + **CITES** (check whether the species is listed on an appendix).
4. **Sub-national variation** where the country devolves animal-keeping (German Länder, Swiss cantons, Belgian regions, ...).

## Primary-law portals by region (most authoritative)
- **United States, federal:** the eCFR (ecfr.gov) + U.S. Code; **USDA APHIS** (Animal Welfare Act dealer/breeder
  licensing; pet-import rules), **USFWS** (the "injurious wildlife" list + wildlife import/export, Form 3-177),
  **CDC** (animal/rodent import restrictions). **Cornell Legal Information Institute** (law.cornell.edu) is a clean
  mirror of the CFR and many state codes.
- **US states:** the state's official legislature / administrative-code portal (search "<state> revised statutes"
  or "<state> administrative code"), with **Cornell LII** as a cross-state mirror. The operative lists/rules come
  from the state **wildlife agency** and the **department of agriculture / board of animal health**.
- **European Union:** **eur-lex.europa.eu** for EU law, and the **EU Invasive Alien Species Union list** (Commission
  Implementing Regulation under Reg. (EU) 1143/2014) to check whether a species is EU-banned.
- **Germany:** **gesetze-im-internet.de** (federal statutes/ordinances), plus the relevant Bundesland portal.
- **Switzerland:** **Fedlex** (fedlex.admin.ch) for federal law; the **canton** issues any actual keeping/breeding permit.
- **Czechia:** zakonyprolidi.cz / e-sbirka, plus the state veterinary administration for trade/movement.
- **Other countries:** the national legislation portal / official gazette (e.g. legislation.gov.uk;
  legislation.gov.au + the state portals; legifrance.gouv.fr for France; normattiva.it / gazzettaufficiale.it for
  Italy). Search "<country> official gazette" or "<country> legislation portal".
- **Reliable SECONDARY (corroborate, never rely on alone):** **animallaw.info** (Michigan State University; strong
  full-text + plain-language summaries across many jurisdictions). Use it to find the citation, then confirm
  against the official text.

## Reliability caveats (learned in the field)
- Some government sites return HTTP 403 to automated requests. Use a cached/mirror copy or a search snippet, then
  confirm the wording against the primary text.
- Deep Research is strong but not infallible: it has miscited section numbers and occasionally pulled "list"
  entries from third-party hobby sites rather than the official list. Always verify the load-bearing claim against
  the primary source.
- An agency's written answer outranks any research summary; record it (RUNBOOK §9) and cite the agency.
- "Not listed" or "the law is silent" is not automatically "unregulated / legal" — first determine the
  jurisdiction's default rule (see the layer checklist and RUNBOOK §6).
